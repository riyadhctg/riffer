# Submission Notes

## Project Approach and AI Usage

Initially, I briefly explored both Riffer and the Mastra.ai workflows documentation. After that, I dug deeper into the Riffer repository to understand its structure and how the existing primitives work. I used AI during this phase to speed up comprehension—for example, by asking comparison questions about how certain ideas differ from LangGraph, which I am more familiar with. It also helped me navigate a few Ruby-specific gotchas as I went deeper into the codebase. Overall, I enjoyed working in Ruby throughout this process and found it a fun language to engage with more hands-on. This exploration happened intermittently alongside other tasks, mainly to build familiarity with the codebase before implementation.

Once I began working on the feature, I started with a rough plan and used AI to brainstorm possible approaches. I also created a small, throwaway “vibe-coded” proof of concept to explore the solution space and better understand the shape of the implementation. After that, I set the POC aside and moved on to building the actual workflow.

During the core implementation phase, AI acted more as an assistant than a driver—helping generate small code snippets, reviewing logic, and suggesting potential edge cases or alternative approaches.

Outside the core implementation, AI was particularly useful in later stages:

- **Testing:** Helped think through the test matrix and generate initial test cases.
- **Documentation & comments:** Assisted with drafting, wording, and improving clarity. For sections like trade-offs, assumptions, and future work, AI primarily helped polish the language.
